10 Downing Street: Behind the Famous Black Door

10 Downing Street is one of the most recognisable addresses in the United Kingdom. Located in the heart of London, it is the official residence and workplace of the British Prime Minister. (1) ______

The building has a long and fascinating history. In 1732, King George II offered the house to Sir Robert Walpole, Britain’s first Prime Minister. However, Walpole did not want to accept it as a personal gift. (2) ______ Since then, the building has been connected with the British government.

From the outside, Number 10 looks like a rather ordinary house with a famous black front door. (3) ______ There are many impressive rooms, beautiful pieces of furniture and valuable works of art.

One of the most interesting parts of the building is the Grand Staircase. Visitors can see portraits of former prime ministers on its walls. Another important room is the Cabinet Room. (4) ______ Its large table has a special shape that allows the Prime Minister to see everyone during meetings.

Number 10 is not only a workplace. The Prime Minister also has private living accommodation inside the building. (5) ______ This makes it possible to combine official responsibilities with everyday life.

The building is also a place where important political decisions are made and international guests are welcomed. (6) ______ For this reason, the famous black door often appears on television and in newspapers around the world.
