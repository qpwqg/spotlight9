01 / LEARN THE RULES

Gerund or Infinitive?

GERUND (–ING)

After certain verbs: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep, practise.

She enjoys reading.
He avoids doing chores.

Also after prepositions: She’s good at cooking.

TO-INFINITIVE

After certain verbs: want, decide, hope, plan, promise, agree, refuse.

I want to travel.
They decided to move.

Also to express purpose: I went out to buy food.

REMEMBER! After modal verbs, use the bare infinitive (without to): You must clean your room. She can cook.
02 / LET’S PRACTISE

Task 1. Complete the sentences

Put the verbs in brackets into the correct form: gerund, to-infinitive or bare infinitive.

  1. Jane enjoys ______ (listen) to music.
  2. They decided ______ (move) to a new house.
  3. I can’t stand ______ (do) the washing-up.
  4. She hopes ______ (visit) London.
  5. Would you mind ______ (help) me?
  6. He refused ______ (clean) his room.
  7. I’m looking forward to ______ (see) you.
  8. You must ______ (finish) your homework.
